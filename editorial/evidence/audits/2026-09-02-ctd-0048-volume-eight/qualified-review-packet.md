## Required reviewer record

Each reviewer must provide:

- Full name.
- Relevant degree, license, appointment, practice, publication, or demonstrated subject qualification.
- Scope accepted and exclusions.
- Conflicts of interest.
- Date reviewed.
- Exact manuscript hash and claim-map version reviewed.
- Sources opened directly rather than accepted from the report.

- Whether any retained statement could reasonably be read as medical or legal advice.

No anonymous, simulated, or editorial-only review satisfies C6 or C7.

## Medical and public-health review

Minimum qualification: a clinician, epidemiologist, toxicologist, public-health researcher, nutrition scientist, reproductive-health specialist, or other person whose training directly covers the accepted scope. More than one reviewer is expected because one person may not be qualified across all topics.

Claims:

- E09 bee pesticide and mite mechanism, if reviewed by a toxicologist whose scope includes ecotoxicology.
- E16 to E18 farmer-suicide count and causal framing.
- E28 to E30 diet, animal protein, cancer, and nutritional consensus.
- E31 to E36 glyphosate, industrial chemicals, microplastics, and radiofrequency exposure.
- P03 to P05 conflict mortality and humanitarian population wording, where public-health methods are involved.
- P27 to P30 suicide and overdose counts, provisional methods, causal explanations, and generalization from the curve.
- P31 and P33 genetic privacy only to the extent of clinical or genetic-data meaning.
- P39 to P42 sperm count, infertility, reproductive exposures, and loneliness.
- Q01 to Q03 sexual frequency and social-time survey interpretation.
- Q07 dark-triad construct and non-diagnostic boundary.

Questions:

1. Does each statistic identify the correct population, denominator, geography, period, and study design?
2. Does the wording preserve the difference among detection, association, hazard, risk, causation, diagnosis, treatment, and health outcome?
3. Are confidence intervals, provisional status, sampling limits, and contested interpretations material enough to appear in the prose?
4. Which claims should be removed because a compact responsible formulation is not possible?
5. Does any sentence overgeneralize from animals, postmortem convenience samples, ecological data, self-report, or aggregate trends?

Claims:

- P31 to P33 23andMe bankruptcy chronology, transferred assets, privacy promises, HIPAA, FTC, and state-law coverage.

- P46 to P50 Ellsberg, Manning, Snowden, and Assange case descriptions and legal consequences.
- Any later Providence wording that asserts legal properties such as privacy, right to deletion, sovereignty, or absence of a landlord.

Questions:

1. Is the statement law, policy, proposal, litigation position, or interpretation, and does the sentence say which?
2. What jurisdiction and effective date govern?

4. Does the prose incorrectly treat absence of HIPAA coverage as absence of legal protection?
5. Does the prose turn an executive directive or proposed bill into present preemption?
6. Does the comparative paragraph equate legally distinct Chinese, European, and United States systems?

## Historical and quotation review

Minimum qualification: a historian of religion, classicist, philologist, folklorist, intellectual historian, or other scholar whose work directly covers the accepted scope. Quotation verification may be completed by an archivist or researcher with access to the primary edition or complete recording.

Claims:

- H01 to H12 Krishnamurti, planetary week, Saturn, festival relations, the birds-and-bees euphemism, and title etymologies.
- E03 and E04 Rosenberg and Carson attribution.
- P01, P02, and P45 to P50 historical comparisons and individual disclosure histories.
- Q04 to Q06 Palantir and Thiel quotations and context.
- Q11 Fuller and Capra quotation and paraphrase lineages.

Questions:

1. Does every cultural claim name its language, region, and period?
2. Does the prose distinguish analogy, later synthesis, adaptation, influence, and documented descent?
3. Is each quotation exact, and is the edition, page, recording, or timestamp attached?
4. Does context materially change the reader's understanding of the quotation?
5. Are modern planetary or festival syntheses being projected backward as uniform ancient systems?
