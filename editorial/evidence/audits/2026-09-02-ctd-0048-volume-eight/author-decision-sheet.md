### D01 Bee loss and pollination

Recommended disposition: revise.

Proposed replacement for the numerical and causal core of the bee paragraph:

> In a survey covering June 2024 through March 2025, 702 United States commercial beekeepers representing 1.835 million colonies reported average losses of about 62 percent, more than 1.1 million colonies among the responding operations. The survey was voluntary and retrospective, so it is evidence about the represented operations rather than a census of every United States hive. Honey-bee pollination adds more than 18 billion dollars to United States crop production each year. Globally, animal pollination supports almost 90 percent of wild flowering-plant species, benefits more than three quarters of leading food-crop types, and contributes to crops that make up about 35 percent of production volume. Those are different denominators, and none is literally a count of every third bite. Recent studies also report interactions between *Varroa* infestation and neonicotinoid exposure under specified conditions, but they do not assign a single cause to the national losses.

### D08 Glyphosate

Recommended disposition: revise to hazard-versus-risk precision.

Proposed replacement:

> In 2015 IARC classified glyphosate as Group 2A, probably carcinogenic to humans, on a hazard-identification record that included limited human evidence for non-Hodgkin lymphoma, sufficient evidence in experimental animals, and strong mechanistic evidence for genotoxicity and oxidative stress. EPA, EFSA, ECHA, and the joint FAO and WHO pesticide-residue process used different evidence rules and exposure questions and did not reach the same regulatory conclusion for authorized uses. The disagreement should be described body by body. A hazard classification is not an estimate of ordinary-use risk, and a regulatory risk finding is not proof that no hazard exists.

### D09 Microplastics and radiofrequency exposure

Recommended disposition: narrow.

Proposed replacement:

> Small postmortem and donated-tissue studies have reported microplastics in human tissues, including all 62 placentas in one New Mexico series and brain samples analyzed in a 2025 *Nature Medicine* paper. The brain study reported higher measured concentrations in 2024 samples than in 2016 samples, but it was not a longitudinal population study and did not establish a clinical effect. Radiofrequency fields belong in a different evidence category: IARC classified them as possibly carcinogenic, Group 2B, while harm at exposures below current guidelines has not been consistently established. In both cases detection, hazard, exposure, and clinical harm must remain separate.

### D11 AI and work

Recommended disposition: revise observations and scenarios separately.

Proposed replacement:

> Through July 2026, Challenger, Gray & Christmas recorded 149,023 announced technology-sector cuts and 112,713 announced cuts in which employers cited AI. Employer attribution is not proof that AI caused every separation. A Stanford Digital Economy Lab analysis of ADP payroll records through June 2026 found a different and narrower pattern: employment among workers aged 22 to 25 in highly AI-exposed occupations was 19 percent below the path of less-exposed peers, with the change concentrated in hiring. The authors found no widespread economy-wide displacement and described the result as non-causal. McKinsey's 2023 United States scenario projected up to 11.8 million additional occupational transitions by 2030, not a measured global job loss.

### D17 Fertility, sperm counts, infertility, and loneliness

Recommended disposition: medical and demographic hold.

Proposed replacement:

> United Nations estimates place global fertility near 2.2 births per woman in 2024, down from about five in the 1960s. Fertility was below 2.1 in 55 percent of countries and areas, representing more than two thirds of the global population. A separate GBD model projects 198 of 204 countries and territories below replacement by 2100; that is a scenario, not destiny. A meta-regression of 223 studies reported large declines in sperm concentration among unselected men between 1973 and 2018, with substantial geographic and methodological limits. WHO estimates that about one in six adults experience infertility in their lifetime, not one in six couples. The 2023 United States Surgeon General's advisory described loneliness and isolation as an epidemic and compared the mortality association of deficient social connection with established risk factors, including smoking. None of these records establishes one common cause.
