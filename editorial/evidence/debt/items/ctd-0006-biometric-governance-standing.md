## Debt

The author has ruled that human judgment may consider demonstrated relational coherence when choosing leaders or stewards. A participant may voluntarily share a private longitudinal record containing biometrics and reflections from others as evidence that their presence has repeatedly supported greater regulation and wellness in people around them. The evidence may inform a decision but cannot decide it alone. Refusing to share it cannot reduce membership, provision, political voice, vote weight, human worth, or human standing. The remaining obligation is to reconcile exact source language, safeguards, equivalent non-biometric routes, consent, validity, contestability, and limits that keep incentives for presence from becoming indirect coercion or biometric gatekeeping.

## Evidence

CTD-0005 and the current corpus already prohibit biometric evidence from determining provision, membership, equal vote weight, human worth, or superior political standing. Volume III gives every participant equal governance standing and vote weight. Volume IV says no sensor or score decides who may speak, vote, or lead. Volume IX preserves bounded stewardship above provision. The 2026-08-22 author clarification resolves the high-level canon question by keeping selection human while permitting a participant to offer private biometric and relational evidence as one voluntary input. The cited sources and the earlier 2026-08-20 candidate still require reconciliation because that candidate categorically excluded biometric personal records from stewardship selection.

## Paydown criteria

- C1. Preserve equal political voice and prohibit biometric-derived evidence from determining provision, membership, speaking or voting rights, chamber eligibility, vote weight, human worth, or superior human or political standing.
- C2. Permit human decision-makers to consider demonstrated relational coherence in bounded leadership or stewardship selection. Allow a participant to offer a voluntarily disclosed private record containing longitudinal biometrics and reflections from others as one input, never as the decision-maker or sole basis for the result.
- C3. Define the relevant claim as whether a person repeatedly helps people around them become more regulated and well, not whether the person's own physiology matches an ideal. Establish role relevance, evidence quality, validation, context, measurement-error, and stopping requirements before any consequential use.
- C4. Ensure that refusing to share private data creates no adverse inference or loss of membership, provision, political voice, vote weight, human worth, or human standing. Provide genuinely accessible non-biometric routes for demonstrating the same role-relevant capacities.
- C5. Define consent and evidence control for the participant and for other people whose biometrics or reflections contribute to the record, including disclosure scope, privacy, correction, revocation, expiry, contestability, due process, and appeal.
- C6. Define incentives for sustained presence and care so they do not become indirect coercion, biometric gatekeeping, a physiological ideal, or discrimination based on disability, neurotype, trauma, health, culture, communication style, or access to measurement.
- C7. Preserve the provisional live-meeting rule: private unanimous meeting-specific consent from everyone present, no activation when a response is readily inferable, ephemeral aggregate non-attributable advisory output, human control, and refusal or withdrawal without penalty.
- C8. Reconcile the cited manuscripts, master ledger, voice cards, audits, and review evidence through the specialist editorial workflow, obtain explicit author approval of the exact source language, and complete every applicable editorial, manuscript, continuity, audio, preview, and publication gate.

## Partial paydown

- Author clarification, 2026-08-22: human judgment may consider demonstrated relational coherence when choosing leaders or stewards.
- Author clarification, 2026-08-22: evidence may include longitudinal biometrics and reflections from others, but only when the participant voluntarily shares their private record.
- Author clarification, 2026-08-22: the relevant question is whether someone repeatedly helps people around them become more regulated and well, not whether their own physiology matches an ideal.
- Author clarification, 2026-08-22: the evidence may inform a decision but cannot decide it alone or become its sole basis.
- Author clarification, 2026-08-22: refusing to share data cannot reduce membership, provision, political voice, vote weight, human worth, or human standing.
- Author clarification, 2026-08-22: communities may reward sustained presence and care, provided those incentives do not become indirect coercion or biometric gatekeeping.
- Author decision, 2026-08-20: a public governance meeting may explore live physiological sensing only when every person present for the sensed portion, including participants and observers, privately and affirmatively consents for that specific meeting. The process reveals only activation or nonactivation and never an individual's response or reason. If a response would be readily inferable, sensing does not activate. Unanimity controls activation of sensing, not whether the meeting proceeds. Refusal, abstention, missing consent, late arrival without consent, or withdrawal leaves sensing off or stops it while the meeting continues without penalty.
- Author decision, 2026-08-20: output must be ephemeral, aggregate, non-attributable, advisory, and excluded from personal records. It cannot automatically pause a meeting or decide an outcome. Any person may request a pause without biometric evidence. Public deliberation does not make physiological data public.
- Reconciliation candidate, 2026-09-01: the dedicated manuscript branch is being updated from the superseded categorical rule to the 2026-08-22 clarification. Fresh review, exact-candidate preview approval, matching immutable audio, remaining validation, and structured closure remain open.
